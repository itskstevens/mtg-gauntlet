How it wins

Lae’zel adds one to every counter you put on a creature or planeswalker. The deck goes wide with creatures and tokens and lets that +1 snowball: [[Cathars' Crusade]], [[Felidar Retreat]], [[Luminarch Aspirant]] and [[Ajani Steadfast]] all do roughly double work. Planeswalkers enter one loyalty higher and every plus ability gives one extra, so the Elspeths, [[Basri Ket]] and the Ajanis reach their ultimates a turn early. Nearly every game ends in combat. At bracket 4, [[Heliod, Sun-Crowned]] plus [[Walking Ballista]] is a backup infinite.

Game plan

  1. T1–2Mana rock or [[Land Tax]], then a one- or two-drop that wants counters: [[Luminarch Aspirant]], [[Ainok Bond-Kin]], [[Esper Sentinel]].
  2. T3Lae’zel. Put [[Swiftfoot Boots]] or [[Lightning Greaves]] on her as soon as you can: she is removed or countered in most games.
  3. T4–5An engine: [[Cathars' Crusade]], [[Felidar Retreat]] or a planeswalker. Add [[Guild Artisan]] once Lae’zel can attack safely for Treasure.
  4. T6+Token makers into Crusade, a protection spell in hand, then swing with [[Ajani Unrelenting]]’s +1 or [[Akroma's Will]].

What to watch for

  • Fast go-wide and aggro decks are the real problem. Against Jetmir, Fynn or Lathril, block early and hold [[Austere Command]] or [[Elspeth, Sun's Champion]]’s −3.
  • Hold [[Teferi's Protection]], [[Flawless Maneuver]] or [[Unbreakable Formation]] until someone casts a wipe, not just until you’re ahead.
  • Never cast [[Walking Ballista]] for X=0 expecting Lae’zel to save it (see Rulings).

How this was run

Each series is a random local-game-store table: three opponents from three different archetype categories, each an EDHREC average deck rolled at bracket 3 or 4, best of three. 100 series per category, 1,800 tables per version, run on the homelab on 4 October 2026.

The simulator is a rule-based AI with no table politics. For this deck it now models Lae’zel’s extra counter, planeswalker loyalty abilities and emblems for all eight walkers, Guild Artisan as a second commander, Cathars’ Crusade, Felidar Retreat, The Ozolith and the Heliod + Ballista loop. Treat small gaps as noise and big ones as direction, not exact numbers.
